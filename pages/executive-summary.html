Findings

  1. The highest-value action is identifier hygiene, not copywriting: the supplied 9-character tokens cannot be used as ASINs or treated as a documented brief-to-product mapping. The observed destination ASINs can be researched as those destinations, and that is the scope of the published source-linked marketing set. None of the supplied links carries an Associates tag, and no public Amazon document binds the short codes to the intended brief products.
  2. Amazon's published economics shape the plan. Only a few bands are high (Luxury Beauty 10.00%; music/handmade/video 5.00%; Books/Kitchen/Automotive 4.50%); the largest consumer clusters sit at 3.00% and the default band is 4.00%, while nine category groups pay 0.00% and are excluded from the niche queue before ranking even starts.
  3. Attribution is narrow and mechanical: a 24-hour Session, an 89-day cart path, and shipment/payment inside 180 days. Content is therefore worth optimising only where readers are close to a purchase decision, and the model credits nothing outside that window.
  4. Prices and ratings may not be self-hosted as static text: they may appear only from an Amazon-served link or a dated API response. That is why a product whose listing could not be fetched shows a blank price cell, and nothing on the site can look like a stale price.
  5. PA-API 5.0 is deprecated (calls return 403 AccessDenied) in favour of the Creators API, which itself requires an Associates account with 10 or more qualifying sales in the last 30 days. Any stack built on the old API fails at runtime; this one degrades to a recorded BLOCKED state instead of scraping.
  6. Reviewing the previously merged commit found no false claims — its four citations are live official pages — but it predated the commission table, the attribution mechanics, per-row sourcing and the category subpages added here.
