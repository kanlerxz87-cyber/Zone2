P-02 · Architecture

One public name and one public site

proposed effort High
What would change
The published tree carries six different site names across its namespaces (measured 2026-11-15 from each page's <title>): Zone2, Counter Notes, Zone2 decisions, Zone2 Revalidation, Zone2 — Amazon Affiliate Intelligence Dashboard, Northstar · Affiliate Intelligence. Choose one public name. Everything else either becomes an internal surface under a clearly labelled path or is preserved as a dated archive.
Why
Well over a hundred HTML pages across a dozen top-level namespaces. A reader cannot tell which one is the site, and neither could a reviewer at Associates reading the application. Google's site-reputation test asks whether content is “fully integrated with the main site” and lists “how the content is presented” — design, formatting, typography, UX — as the first objective factor. Six names reads as six sites.
Options
  • A. One public brand; other namespaces moved under an /archive path and excluded from search recommendedRecommended. Largest cleanup, and the only option that leaves a reader with one answer to “what site am I on?”
  • B. Two surfaces: one public brand, one clearly internal consoleCheaper. Works only if the internal one is honestly labelled and not indexed.
  • C. Keep all sixNo work now; every future content decision has to be made six times.
Risk
Archive moves change URLs. Sitemaps and internal links must be regenerated in the same commit or the site ships broken links.

P-03 · Editorial trust

A named byline and a “how this was written” line on every article

proposed effort Low
What would change
Every article carries a named human author or responsible editor, plus one dated line stating how the piece was produced and what it was checked against.
Why
Measured 2026-11-15: no page in any namespace names a person. Google lists “Its stated or implied authorship, meaning, is there an explicit acknowledgement of ownership or responsibility for the content?” as one of four objective factors it weighs, and its guidance on generated content says sharing how a piece was made “can help give your readers more context”. The FTC requires the commercial connection to be disclosed clearly and conspicuously; an unnamed page makes that harder to judge.
Options
  • A. Named editor plus a dated provenance line on every article recommendedRecommended. Cheap, and it is the single cheapest signal that the site is edited rather than emitted.
  • B. Publication name only, no individualBetter than nothing; does not satisfy the authorship factor as written.
  • C. No bylineStatus quo.
Risk
The name must be a person who accepts responsibility. A invented byline would be worse than none — see Q-16.
