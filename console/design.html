Redesign pack

Why the site reads as machine output, and the concrete rewrite that fixes it — with every hard constraint traced to the official page that imposes it.

Read the diagnosis first. Everything in this pack follows from one finding: the site describes itself instead of answering the reader. Fixing the tone without fixing that produces better-sounding copy about a project status report.

Diagnosis

The site described itself instead of answering the reader. A redesign merged mid-review has fixed the worst of it; two traces remain.

Measured 2026-11-15 from the published pages. At the start of this review the root landing page opened its executive summary with the client's own requirement list, verbatim (“The job. Build an affiliate marketing platform that researches products, finds trending and high-converting niches, analyzes pricing …”), and its headline figure strip reported the project's audit state (“3 of 3 Supplied links that resolve…”, “0 of 3 Brief product mappings confirmed by an official Amazon document (F-03)”, count of items on the Flagged register). CORRECTION, same day: session f8e18ca8's redesign (PR #108) merged while this review was running and removed both. The page now opens “Affiliate content you can check, not just read.” Two traces of the same problem remain: the “AI-powered Amazon affiliate platform” heading, and the section heading “Three links in. Three listings identified.”, which is broader than what flag F-03 confirms.

Not because the sentences are badly written — they are not. Because every sentence was about the site rather than about the reader's problem. Machine-sounding copy is usually copy with no stake in it: it describes, it does not choose. The redesign proves the point from the other direction: the moment the page started answering a reader, it stopped sounding like machine output. What is left is the same failure in two headlines instead of a whole page.

2. Voice

Do

  • Open with the answer, in one sentence, in plain words.
  • Name the trade-off out loud. “Two baskets means two things finish together and two things to wash.”
  • Use the number you actually have. “6 Qt, in two 3 Qt baskets” beats “generous capacity”.
  • Write to one reader: a household cooking for two to four people on a normal counter.
  • Use short sentences after long ones. Vary length; do not perform it.
  • Say when two official pages disagree, and say which one you used and why.
  • Put the limitation in the same paragraph as the claim.

7. Design system

Type

  • Body: System sans, 18px / 1.65, measure 62-68 characters
  • Display: One serif face for headlines only, 40/48/32/24px steps
  • Mono: Used for ASINs, source IDs and table keys only

Colour

One accent for links and verdicts. Neutral greys for everything else. No gradients, no glassmorphism, no coloured KPI tiles.

The KPI-tile look is what makes the current site read as a dashboard. Text and one accent read as an article.

Layout

Single reading column, 720px. Tables may break out to the full width. No card grids above the fold.

Card grids are a directory pattern. A buyer comparing three things needs a table, not a gallery.

Components

  • Verdict box — one sentence, top of a product page, before anything else
  • Source chip — a small link with the host name, at the end of every table row
  • Open-item box — what could not be verified, dated, at the foot of the page
  • Provenance line — author, check date, verification date, disclosure; one line, foot of the article
  • Comparison table — only the rows that differ

Components that must not exist

  • A price component of any kind
  • A star-rating component of any kind
  • A price-alert or price-drop component of any kind

8. The brief, ready to paste

Everything above, condensed into one brief that can be handed to any writer, designer or model. Constraints marked [rule] are quoted from official sources and may not be relaxed; [craft] is house style.

590 words
You are writing and designing one page of an Amazon affiliate site about three countertop air fryers.

THE READER
One person cooking for two to four people on a normal kitchen counter, deciding which of three machines to buy. They arrived from a search. They have not heard of this site and do not care how it is built.

HARD RULES [rule] — these come from Amazon's Operating Agreement and Program Policies and Google's published spam policies; they cannot be relaxed.
1. The page must state, clearly and prominently: “As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.”
2. Print no product price and no star rating. Prices are allowed only if Amazon serves the link containing them or the data comes from Creators API; ratings only through Creators API. Neither is available. Where a price or rating would go, print a dash and one sentence explaining why.
3. Never build price tracking or price alerts.
4. Make no inaccurate, overbroad, deceptive or misleading claim about any product. No “best”, no “cheapest”, no superlative you cannot defend from a row on the page.
5. Make no claim about expected traffic or commission income.
6. Do not copy the listing's or the manufacturer's description. Add something, or do not write the paragraph.
7. Do not send the reader straight to an Amazon URL, or through a redirect. Links go to Amazon product pages from this page, one click, rel="sponsored".
8. Every outbound link must be clearly a link to Amazon — no cloaked URLs, no hidden destinations.
9. No claim may appear without the row that justifies it and the official page that row came from. A value with no source is a dash, never a guess.
10. Nothing may be published until a named person has fact-checked it against its sources.

CRAFT RULES [craft] — house style, not policy.
11. Open with the answer. One sentence, under 25 words, before any context.
12. Delete any sentence that would still be true if you swapped the product name.
13. Write to one reader in the second person. No “whether you're a busy parent or a professional chef”.
14. Use the number you actually have. “6 Qt in two 3 Qt baskets”, not “generous capacity”.
15. Name the trade-off in the same breath as the benefit.
16. Never use: in today's fast-paced world; when it comes to; it's important to note that; look no further; game-changer; revolutionary; cutting-edge; seamless; effortless; elevate; unlock; leverage; delve into; the ultimate guide; the comprehensive guide; let's take a closer look.
17. No card grids above the fold. One reading column. A table where a comparison belongs.
18. Say what you could not find out, dated, on the page.

PAGE STRUCTURE
Product page: verdict (1 sentence) → who it suits and who it does not → the three numbers that separate it → full specification table with a source link per row → where the official pages disagree → what we could not verify → byline, how it was checked, disclosure.
Question guide: the reader's question as the headline → the answer in the first two sentences → the reasoning from sourced rows → the exception, when the answer is the other one → links to the two product pages this answer points at → byline, how it was checked, disclosure.

BEFORE YOU FINISH
Re-read the page and delete every sentence that describes the site, the process, the sources or the verification effort rather than the product. Then check every number against its row. Then sign it.
