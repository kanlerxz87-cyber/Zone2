Affiliate disclosure. As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.

Design brief

Executive summary · measured 2026-11-16

The research platform is finished and it is honest. The earning part has not started, and it cannot start until the owner supplies one string.

The blocking fact

Nothing on this site can earn money today, and the fix is one string the owner has to supply.

Amazon requires Associates tags to be embedded in the product links on the site declared in the application, and it reviews an application only once at least three qualifying sales have been driven within the first 180 days. This site has zero tagged links, so it has zero qualifying purchases, so the application cannot be reviewed. Meanwhile the three links the whole project was built around are themselves short links carrying no tag, so even the supplied links would not attribute a sale to the owner. Every downstream capability in the brief — conversion tracking, the optimizer, the revenue model, the API enrolment that would let the site publish prices and ratings — sits behind this one missing value.

Decide these three first

Decide 1

DB-06 — Declare one site, and demote the other thirteen to linked evidence

Declaring one site determines what every other change is a change to. It is also the only decision that has to be right before an application names a URL, because an application is not reassessed after a rejection.

Decide 2

BQ-14 — What is the Associates ID (tag) and which marketplace is it registered in?

Supplying the Associates tag is the owner's one action that unblocks the entire revenue side. It takes one sign-in and no code.

Decide 3

DB-01 — Stop describing the site as AI-powered on the operator and companion pages

It costs under an hour and it is reversible. The T55 rebuild removed the phrase from every reader-facing page; 14 operator and companion pages still carry it.

What was asked

  • Build an AI-powered Amazon affiliate platform: product research, trend and conversion analysis, pricing, review, competition and commission analysis, SEO articles, comparisons and buying guides, niche discovery, link and campaign management, click and conversion tracking, continuous optimization — automated, scalable, data-driven, and compliant with Amazon Associates policy and disclosure rules.
  • Build the marketing for three supplied affiliate links.
  • Make it look like a polished marketing site rather than machine output, with articles that earn organic traffic.
  • Provide a suggestions board where a path is chosen before anything is implemented, and a clear register of what is known, what is not, and how to find out.
  • Provide an executive summary.

What is settled

  • The three supplied links resolve, on four separate dates across four sessions, to the same three listings: a Chefman 6-quart dual basket, an Emeril Lagasse 26-quart French-door oven, and a Chefman 8-quart basket fryer (BR-19, BR-20, BR-21).
  • The evidence discipline holds at scale: 192 of 192 pages carry the disclosure, no page prints a product price, and every specification row links to the page it was read from.
  • The machinery is trustworthy: 51 of 51 workflow steps pass locally, and the counts that matter are derived from the tree rather than typed.
  • The rules are known and quoted: what may be published, what may not, and where the disclosure has to sit.

What is not

  • Whether the three listings are the products the brief meant. Nothing in any official document binds a supplied code to a product (BQ-18).
  • Whether an Associates account exists at all, and if it does, its tag and marketplace (BQ-14, BQ-19).
  • Which of the 19 published surfaces is the site that would be declared (BQ-15).
  • Who writes it, under what name, and whether that name is public (BQ-16).
  • What readers are searching for. There is no demand signal anywhere in the project, so no topic on the topics board can be ranked by expected volume (BQ-20).

The one thing

If only one thing happens next, it should be the tag. Everything the brief asks the platform to do with real traffic — track it, convert it, optimize against it — is unreachable until a link on a page can be attributed to the owner.
