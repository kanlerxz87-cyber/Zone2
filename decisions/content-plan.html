The content plan

The "does not read like AI slop" requirement, restated as the four things Google actually asks for, with a plan that starts from the evidence this project already owns.

12 proposals · 0 implemented
"AI slop" is a real, defined complaint, not a matter of tasteGoogle's documentation describes the failure precisely: content produced by generative AI 'without manual oversight or curation' represents 'little to no effort', and 'using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users may violate' the scaled-content-abuse policy. So the fix is not better prose — it is adding work the machine did not do.

Why this site currently reads as machine output

  • Everything is a table. The layout is honest and unusual, and honesty is the site's best asset — but a page of sourced cells makes no argument, and an argument is what a reader came for.
  • No human made a judgement anywhere. Every sentence is either a quoted requirement or a rendered value. A reader can feel the absence of a person choosing between two products and saying why.
  • The strongest material is buried. The three published spec conflicts — the 1000 W vs 1700 W power discrepancy and the 24 vs 25 cooking-function discrepancy — are the most useful and most original things this project produced, and they are recorded as flags rather than written up as the reason to read the site.
  • No byline, no date-of-a-person, no voice. Google asks who created the content and whether that is self-evident; on this site it is not evident at all.

The plan, in the order that needs no Amazon account first

  1. Write three pieces that carry an argument. A decision-first buying guide (organised by kitchen constraint, not by product), a capacity explainer that uses the verified 6 Qt-in-two-3-Qt-baskets figure as its worked example, and a 'what the marketing copy hides' piece built from the spec conflicts already in the ledger. This is original analysis of evidence the project already owns, and it is the only content on the site that a copy-farm competitor could not reproduce. The article-format choice itself belongs to the canonical board (SG-04).
  2. Publish an author or an editorial statement. One honest sentence about how the site is made beats an invented persona, which Google explicitly calls deception. The voice question is SG-05 on the canonical board.
  3. Put the paid-link label next to every affiliate link. Two hours, and it closes the gap measured in RF-02: 7 pages carry an outbound affiliate anchor, only 3 carry the in-context label.
  4. Then measure, and only then expand. Without impressions and queries the next article is a guess, and guessing is how the generic set got written. Measurement is SG-09 on the canonical board.
  5. Do not write about products nobody has handled in a voice that implies otherwise. The FTC states you cannot talk about your experience with a product you have not tried; the project has tested nothing, and the plan is built to stay true to that.

What would make this page unnecessary

A page per decision that a reader cannot get anywhere else, written by someone whose name is on it, based on evidence a content farm would not have gathered. The repository's machinery already gathers that evidence and gates it; what it has never had is a writer, a byline and a measurement of whether anyone reads the result. Those are the three gaps this plan closes.
