Thirteen choices, each with its evidence in the same box.

How to read this page

  • Each row is one thing that could change, the evidence for changing it, what it costs, what it gives up, and the single answer that unblocks it.
  • A row marked 'open' is waiting on the owner. Nothing on this page applies itself, and nothing on it is implemented by being written here.
  • Rows are grouped by the question a reader would ask, not by the department that would do the work.

Every claim in an evidence list links to its source. Where the source is a measurement this session took, the link is to the dated verification record, which states the method and the raw counts. Where the source is an official document, it is the document itself. Nothing here converts either into a claim about demand, popularity or revenue.

Findability · DR-01

Open — waiting on the owner

Delete or redirect the duplicate shop front door at shop.html

Two different shop home pages are live. Which one should exist?

What the measurement shows

  • shop.html and shop/index.html are two separate designs with different h1 text ("Find the format that fits your routine." vs "Five pieces of kitchen gear, explained before you buy") and ~3% text similarity. Measured: the packaged _site artifact, robots + canonical + sitemap ↗
  • shop/index.html is linked from 52 published pages; shop.html from 15 — including the front page hero and every product card button. Measured: the packaged _site artifact, robots + canonical + sitemap ↗
  • Only shop.html is in sitemap.xml and marked index,follow. shop/index.html — the one 52 pages link to — is noindex,follow and absent from the sitemap. Measured: the packaged _site artifact, robots + canonical + sitemap ↗
  • Google consolidates duplicates by canonical and sitemap signals; two near-duplicate commerce landing pages compete for the same intent. Google Search Central — Consolidate duplicate URLs (canonical) ↗

What the options cost

Nothing here is applied by being listed. Each option lists what it costs and what it gives up, in this project's judgement, against the governing rule for this row: Google Search Central — Consolidate duplicate URLs (canonical) ↗

A Keep shop/index.html, redirect shop.html to itRecommended

Costs: One rule in the packager; ~15 internal links to shop.html keep working through the redirect.

Gives up: Nothing. shop.html's design is the weaker of the two.

B Keep shop.html, retire shop/index.html

Costs: 52 pages of internal links must be rewritten; the shop section's own category pages lose their parent.

Gives up: The shop section's richer per-product spec pages.

C Leave both, differentiate them deliberately

Costs: Two pages to maintain and two sets of metadata; still two front doors for one intent.

Gives up: Clarity, and any claim that the site has one canonical shop.

Our recommendation — option A

shop/index.html is the page 52 pages already point at, and the page whose children (categories, per-product pages, shop-local guides) form a coherent section. Making the sitemap advertise the page nobody links to is backwards.

Unblocks with: One answer from the owner: which shop URL should be canonical. Then this is a packager rule plus a regenerated sitemap.

Findability · DR-02

Open — waiting on the owner

Index the product and buying-guide pages that currently answer nothing to search

The deepest verified content on this site is invisible to search engines. Should it be indexed?

What the measurement shows

  • The deployed artifact has 16 index,follow pages and 212 noindex pages. Measured: the packaged _site artifact, robots + canonical + sitemap ↗
  • shop/chefman-6-qt-dual-basket-air-fryer.html is noindex and carries 802 words, 2 tables, 20 sourced rows and 16 official source links. The indexable guides/chefman-dual-basket.html carries 334 words and 9 source links. Measured: the packaged _site artifact, robots + canonical + sitemap ↗
  • Same pattern for the ThermoMaven: 640 noindex words with 20 rows vs 339 indexable words with 7 source links. Measured: the packaged _site artifact, robots + canonical + sitemap ↗
  • Affiliate pages are held to a thin-affiliation standard: they must offer meaningful original content beyond product links. Google Search Central — Spam policies: thin affiliation ↗
  • A sitemap should list the URLs you want indexed. Google Search Central — Build and submit a sitemap ↗

What the options cost

Nothing here is applied by being listed. Each option lists what it costs and what it gives up, in this project's judgement, against the governing rule for this row: Google Search Central — Build and submit a sitemap ↗

A Index the five product pages and the shop-local guidesRecommended

Costs: Moves ~12 URLs from noindex to index; each must pass the existing disclosure, sponsorship and sourcing gates on its own.

Gives up: The current, very clean 16-page indexing surface.

B Keep the 16-page surface; strengthen those pages instead

Costs: Editorial work to fold the 20-row tables into the guides that are indexable.

Gives up: The section structure already built in shop/.

C Index product pages only, keep guides noindex

Costs: Splits the section's search surface by page type and needs a reason per type.

Gives up: Simplicity.

Our recommendation — option A

The noindex boundary was drawn to keep operator dashboards out of search, and it does that. But it also swept up the most substantive reader pages, which is the opposite of the intent. The sourcing gates already exist and run over shop/**.

Unblocks with: Owner approval of an indexing surface of roughly 28 URLs, plus a decision on which shop pages are reader pages versus owner pages.

Pick one feed, and stop publishing the same five questions at ten URLs

Three feeds are live, and all five questions in the root feed are repeated in the shop feed at different URLs. Which feed is the site's feed?

What the measurement shows

  • Three self-describing feeds are published: feed.xml (5 guide items), shop/feed.xml (6) and marketing/feed.xml (16). Measured: item-level overlap of feed.xml and shop/feed.xml ↗
  • All five questions in feed.xml also appear in shop/feed.xml at a different URL. Two carry a byte-identical title ("Basket or oven-style air fryer: what should you compare?" and "A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity"); the other three are reworded versions of the same question, e.g. "How should you assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs?" vs "How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs". Measured: item-level overlap of feed.xml and shop/feed.xml ↗
  • So the site asks a subscriber to receive the same five answers twice, under ten different URLs, from two feeds. Measured: item-level overlap of feed.xml and shop/feed.xml ↗
  • Two URLs with the same content split the signals a search engine uses to decide which one to rank. Google Search Central — Consolidate duplicate URLs (canonical) ↗

What the options cost

Nothing here is applied by being listed. Each option lists what it costs and what it gives up, in this project's judgement, against the governing rule for this row: Google Search Central — Consolidate duplicate URLs (canonical) ↗

A One feed (feed.xml), one URL per guide, 301 the otherRecommended

Costs: Retire shop/feed.xml and the shop-local duplicate guide URLs; keep the shop guide pages only where they add rows the root guide lacks.

Gives up: The shop section's self-contained feed.

B Three feeds, but no duplicated questions

Costs: Each feed keeps a distinct purpose, and every guide exists at exactly one URL.

Gives up: Nothing, but it needs a rule the build can enforce.

C Leave it; the feeds are for different audiences

Costs: Nothing now.

Gives up: The ability to say the site publishes each answer once.

Our recommendation — option A

A reader who subscribes to the site's feed should not receive the same question twice under different URLs. One feed and one URL per guide is the version that survives contact with a search engine.

Unblocks with: Which feed the site's <link rel=alternate> should point at. Everything else follows mechanically.

The link that earns money is an 18-pixel-tall text link on a phone. Should it be a button?

What the measurement shows

  • "View on Amazon ↗ paid link" measures 142 x 18 CSS px at 390px wide, 63px from its sibling link. Measured: pointer target boxes and spacing at 390px ↗
  • SC 2.5.8 asks for 24 x 24 CSS px, but accepts undersized targets that are sufficiently spaced. At 63px spacing this passes the letter of the criterion. W3C — Understanding SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), Level AA ↗
  • The criterion's own note encourages authors to meet the minimum size regardless of spacing, especially for important links. W3C — Understanding SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), Level AA ↗
