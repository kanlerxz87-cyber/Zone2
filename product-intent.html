Product-intent confirmation record

Five link.amazon codes were submitted to this project. This page answers, and keeps separate, two different questions: what each link was meant to promote (the owner's claim, confirmed from a source), and where each link actually went when this project opened it (dated measurements). Nothing in the second table is ever copied into the first.

Why separate. Amazon's ASIN definition (S24) makes an intended ASIN a checkable claim — ten characters, one catalog record — while a nine-character short-link code is not an identifier anything can check. A register that merged intent with observation would let a redirect guess become a fact. Rule (brief, 2026-11-17): do not merge the two fields.

Flagged for review

The open items this record depends on, with their source links, on the shared register.

  • F-42 — two of the five submitted codes open meat-thermometer listings, not air fryers. The owner's answer decides the intended fields above and the site's category scope.
  • F-03 — no Amazon document binds a link.amazon code to a catalog record; the intended fields cannot be derived from the observed ones.
  • F-01 — the submitted codes are nine characters; an ASIN is ten. The codes are opaque short-link identifiers (F-28's control), and none is padded or repaired here.
  • F-43 — the tagged Special Links pass the destination check; the tracking-report check needs the owner's Associates Central session.
  • F-24 — the supplied short links carry no tag; the tagged Special Links this site publishes are separate objects (ledger/site_config.json) and do not rewrite the supplied ones.

How the blanks get filled

  1. Owner confirms intent. For each submitted code, the owner states the intended ASIN (10 characters) or the intended product page, in Associates Central or in writing.
  2. Record the source and date. The confirmation date and where it came from go in the same row; only then do the intended cells fill.
  3. Then, and only then, may editorial copy describe a submitted link as promoting a named product. Until that moment every surface says what is observed and marks intent unverified.
