DG-02Link strategy
Why it costs: The brief asks the platform to 'publish and manage affiliate links' and 'track clicks and conversions'. As supplied, none of the three can attribute a commission to any account, so no tracking or optimization layer has a link to act on.
Recommended: Publish direct, fully visible product URLs now (already the case), and replace them with tagged Special Links in one step when the owner supplies an Associates ID. Never republish the short links. See DS-02 and SG-13.
The three links in the brief are link.amazon short links. They cannot serve as this site's Special Links: an Associates ID must appear as a `tag` parameter on every Special Link, the supplied links carry none, and republishing a shortener whose destination is unclear is separately restricted.
How it was measured: Re-observed 2026-11-15: each of the three URLs redirects to https://www.amazon.com/dp/<ASIN> with `plpRedirect=mhFallback` and a per-fetch `btn_ref` token, and no `tag=` parameter (C-17 in the existing ledger; reproduced by this session).

DG-07Pricing
Why it costs: A price is the strongest single conversion lever on an affiliate page, and its absence is the second-largest revenue cost after the missing tag. Any plan that adds prices has to satisfy S54 first.
Recommended: Do not add prices, price history or price alerts. Prepare a dated, API-sourced price display as a proposal blocked on the account and API access. See DS-07 and SG-10.
The site shows no price, and cannot: Amazon only permits a price when it is served on the link or supplied through Creators API / PA API, and the site has neither. Price tracking and alerting are separately forbidden without Amazon's written agreement.
How it was measured: Read from the live policy pages (S53, S54). The repository's own gate forbids printing a price in a buying context, and the only figure the site publishes is the program's 'up to 10%' commission ceiling (S37).

DG-01Product presentation
Why it costs: A shop window with no pictures is the single largest conversion cost on the site, and Google's guidance names 'additional information', 'rigorous testing and ratings' and 'original reviews' as what makes an affiliate page worth ranking (S48). A reader deciding between three air fryers wants to see the door, the basket and the footprint.
Recommended: Keep the site image-free until Program Content is available, and close the layout gap now with original, self-drawn vector diagrams of the measured specifications (capacity, footprint, door geometry) that belong to this project and carry no product photograph. See DS-01.
What cannot be done about it: Product photographs may come only from Program Content under the IP License, may not be stored or cached, and a link to one may be kept for at most 24 hours (S45). No Associates account exists (F-07), so no Program Content is available to this project today. Copying a manufacturer's or a retailer's photograph is not permitted by that licence and is not proposed anywhere in this register.
No page on the site shows a product image. Measured on 2026-11-15: 0 <img> elements across all 116 published HTML pages.
How it was measured: grep -c '<img' over every *.html in the repository (root 17, pages/ 25, marketing/ 18, journal/ 4, companion namespaces) returns 0 matched elements.

DG-03Organic traffic
Why it costs: The brief asks the platform to 'generate organic traffic so real people can click the links and buy'. More pages is the intuitive answer and the wrong one: scaled unoriginal pages are a spam-policy violation (S47) and thin affiliate pages are demoted (S48). The only durable route is pages that contain something a competitor's page does not have.
Recommended: Grow on the assets already gathered: one page per decision question, each answering it from the published numbers, and a comparison engine built from the same ledger. See DS-03 and SG-08/SG-09.
The site cannot grow by publishing more pages. Its own editorial rules forbid the unsourced claims a content farm would use, and Google's spam policies demote exactly that shape of page.
How it was measured: Read the current gate set: tools/validate_data.py plus marketing/scripts/verify_marketing.py reject unsourced values, filler phrasing and first-hand claims. The site's product pages are dense specification tables, which is the 'meaningful content' S48 asks for, but only four editorial pieces exist outside them (journal/, three articles).

DG-04Organic traffic

DG-06Structured data
Why it costs: Star ratings in search results come from Review/AggregateRating markup, and Google's guidelines forbid aggregating ratings from other sites or marking up reviews nobody wrote (S50, S60). An air fryer comparison without star ratings looks less competitive than a competitor's, so it is worth being explicit that the gap is a compliance consequence, not laziness.
Recommended: Do not add rating markup. Add the structured data that is honest today (Article, BreadcrumbList, WebSite) to the root dashboard and pages/, and revisit Product markup only if measured data ever exists. See DS-06.
No page carries Product, Review or AggregateRating structured data, and none should yet: the site has tested nothing.
How it was measured: grep for application/ld+json: 18 pages in marketing/ carry Article + BreadcrumbList only; the root dashboard and pages/ carry none. No Product or AggregateRating object exists anywhere in the tree.

DG-08Specification accuracy
Why it costs: The honest table is a strength; leaving the reader to arbitrate is a decision they are poorly equipped to make. Explaining the arbitration rule in one sentence is both more useful and more defensible than picking silently.
Recommended: State a single, published arbitration rule on the verification page and link it from every conflict row: the manufacturer's current product page wins for specifications, and the listing text is shown as what the retailer says. No value changes. See DS-08.
Four specification rows are published as disagreements between two official pages, and the site is right to publish both numbers. But a reader is not told which number to trust when buying.
How it was measured: The published conflicts: Emeril cooking functions (24 in the Amazon listing title vs 25 presets on the manufacturer tile), Chefman 8 Qt power (1000 W in an Amazon bullet vs 1700 W on the manufacturer page), and two Chefman 8 Qt dimension rows that differ between the two pages. DG-10Authorship and trust
Why it costs: Google's people-first guidance asks who created the content and how, and explicitly warns that fabricated creator profiles are deception while background about automation is useful (S56). The site's whole method is automation with a human fact-check gate, which is a genuinely good story it currently does not tell.

DG-11Measurement
Why it costs: The brief asks for automation, scalability and data-driven decisions. Without a measurement layer every future decision stays a judgement, and the site's own pages say so rather than pretending.
Recommended: Ship a measurement layer that is policy-safe by construction: a static, cookie-free, first-party event schema with no personal data, plus a documented plan for Search Console and for Associates reporting once the account exists. Do not publish a traffic or earnings forecast (S58). See DS-11 and SG-11.
The platform claims to 'track clicks and conversions and continuously optimize campaigns'. No click, conversion or earnings data is collected anywhere, and none could be: there is no tagged link, no analytics property and no Associates account.
How it was measured: The repository's own validator forbids a populated clicks/conversion/earnings field. No analytics snippet, no Search Console verification file and no first-party event log exists in the tree.

DG-14Program Content
Why it costs: If any quoted listing text is Product Advertising Content, the required disclaimer is missing on every page that shows it. If it is not, the disclaimer is harmless. The cost of being wrong is asymmetric, and the fix is one line of text on pages that quote listing text.
Recommended: Add the 2(k) disclaimer verbatim to pages that display Amazon-sourced text, and record it in the compliance ledger. This is a text change with no data effect. See DS-14 and RF-02.
The site quotes Amazon listing titles and bullets extensively. The IP License requires a plain-view disclaimer on any display of Product Advertising Content consisting of text.
How it was measured: S45 Section 2(k) requires the sentence beginning 'CERTAIN CONTENT THAT APPEARS ... COMES FROM AMAZON.' to be included in plain view. grep across all published HTML for 'CERTAIN CONTENT' returns no match.
