What could change, and what each choice costs

This is the review board. 14 proposals, each with two or three real options, the trade-off of every option, the evidence behind it, and the questions that block it. Nothing here has been built. 14 of the 14 are waiting on a decision you record — that is the intended state, not an incomplete one.

Read the options, pick one per row, and the choice becomes the instruction. Where a recommendation is compound ("A, then B"), it means: do A now and revisit B when the blocker clears.

Start here if you only read three. DS-02 decides what the product links point at — the difference between a site that can earn and one that cannot. DS-03 decides how traffic is earned without tripping the scaled-content rules. DS-11 decides whether there will ever be data to base the next choice on.

How should the product pages show what the products look like, without a licence to use Amazon photography?

DS-01Product presentationAWAITING_DECISIONBlocked by: DQ-01, DQ-02, DQ-03

Leave every photograph out. Replace the empty visual slot with original, self-drawn SVG diagrams generated from the specification rows the site already publishes: a capacity diagram showing the 6 Qt two-basket interior against the 8 Qt single chamber, a footprint diagram drawn to scale from the published dimensions, and a door-geometry diagram for the French-door oven. Each diagram carries a caption naming the source row it was drawn from.

Why: A reader needs to see the shape of the decision, and the two hard constraints (no Program Content without an account; no storing an Amazon image) do not stop the site drawing its own accurate diagram of a measured number. A diagram drawn to scale from a sourced dimension is original work and carries no Amazon image, no Amazon mark and no third-party photograph.

  • AOriginal specification diagrams, no photographs recommendedCompliant today with no account, and genuinely useful for capacity and fit. Costs drawing work, and a diagram is less persuasive than a photograph.
  • BWait for an Associates account, then use Program Content images recommendedThe most persuasive option, but blocked on DQ-01/DQ-03 and possibly on a static host satisfying the no-cache rule. Nothing ships until the account exists.
  • CNothing visual; keep tables onlyZero effort and zero risk. Leaves the largest conversion cost on the site in place.

What should the site link to while there is no Associates tag?

DS-02Link strategyAWAITING_DECISIONBlocked by: DQ-01

Publish full, visible amazon.com/dp/<ASIN> URLs with a clear label, the '(paid link)' tag beside the link and rel="sponsored nofollow", exactly as now, and add one documented switch that turns every product link into a tagged Special Link the moment an Associates ID exists. Document on the flagged page why the three supplied short links are not used.

Why: A full product URL is unambiguous and cannot be mistaken for a cloaked redirect (S46, S52). It is also the only shape that becomes a Special Link by adding a parameter, which makes the switch a one-line data change rather than a rewrite.

  • ADirect visible product URLs now, documented switch for the tag recommendedCompliant and honest; earns nothing until the tag exists, and the site must keep saying so.
  • BPublish the three supplied short linksNot recommended. They carry no tag, the shortener is a link-shortening service, and a short URL makes the destination unclear (S46).
  • CPublish placeholder tagged links that are switched on laterWorkable, but a placeholder tag is easy to ship by accident and any invented tag is a compliance problem.

How should the site earn organic traffic without tripping the scaled-content rules?

DS-03Organic trafficAWAITING_DECISIONNot blocked: can start as soon as you choose

Publish one page per buying question, each answering it from the specifications already gathered, and make the four measured cross-source disagreements the flagship pieces. Start with six: how many quarts a household actually needs; two baskets or one chamber; basket fryer or French-door oven; what Hi-Fry changes; will it fit the counter; and why two official pages disagree about one product. Every page states its answer in the first two sentences and cites the row it came from.

Why: These six are exactly the queries a buyer types, and the site already holds the data to answer them with numbers a competitor's page does not have. That is the 'original content or added value' S48 names, and it is the opposite of the many-thin-pages pattern S47 penalises.

  • ASix decision pages from existing data, then pause and measure recommendedHighest value per page and no new fact-gathering. Six pages is not a traffic strategy on its own; it needs the measurement layer to know what to write next.
  • BGenerate 100+ pages across many nichesNot recommended. Without new data per page this is the scaled-content pattern S47 describes, and the brief's own 'no AI slop' complaint is about exactly this outcome.
  • CWrite nothing until traffic data existsPerfectly safe and self-defeating: with no new pages and no analytics, there is no traffic and no data to wait for.

What structured data should the site emit today, given it has tested nothing?

DS-06Structured dataAWAITING_DECISIONNot blocked: can start as soon as you choose

Emit WebSite and BreadcrumbList on the root dashboard, keep Article and BreadcrumbList on editorial pages, and emit no Product, Review or AggregateRating object anywhere. Add a gate that fails the build if Product or AggregateRating markup appears without a measured rating behind it.

Why: Structured data must describe content a reader can see, and ratings must not be aggregated from other sites (S50, S60). A gate is the only way that stays true after the next contributor.

  • AWebSite + BreadcrumbList + Article only, gated recommendedHonest and durable. No star ratings in search results, so the listing looks plainer than a competitor's.
  • BAdd AggregateRating from Amazon listings to win starsNot recommended. Google's guidelines forbid aggregating ratings from other websites (S50), and Amazon forbids displaying star ratings obtained outside the API (S44).
  • CAdd nothing anywhereNo risk, no benefit; loses the disambiguation and breadcrumb treatment that costs nothing to emit.

Should the site show prices at all?

DS-07PricingAWAITING_DECISIONBlocked by: DQ-01, DQ-02

Show no price, no price history and no price alert. Prepare a dated price display that is switched on only when Amazon serves it on the link or a Creators API response supplies it, with the required timestamp and the standard accuracy disclaimer. Ship the preparation as documented code with the switch off.

Why: S54 permits a price only from those two sources; S53 forbids tracking and alerting without written agreement. Preparing the compliant path costs nothing and makes the eventual switch a configuration change rather than a project.

  • BDisplay manufacturer prices as a proxyNot recommended. A manufacturer price is not the price at checkout and S54 governs the Amazon price a reader will actually pay.
  • CAdd price history charts and price-drop alertsNot available without Amazon's written agreement (S53), and would be a policy violation if shipped now.

When two official pages disagree, which number should the reader trust?

DS-08Specification accuracyAWAITING_DECISIONBlocked by: DQ-07

Publish one arbitration rule and apply it everywhere: the manufacturer's current product page governs a specification value; the retail listing is quoted as what the retailer states, and the difference is shown rather than hidden. State the rule on the verification page and link it from every conflict row. Change no value.

Why: The site already shows both numbers, which is honest but shifts the decision to the reader (DG-08). A published rule is more useful and more defensible, and it costs no new research.

  • AManufacturer page governs specifications; listing quoted as-is recommendedClear and consistent. A manufacturer can be wrong or out of date about its own product, and the Amazon listing may reflect a revision the maker's page has not caught.
  • BNewest-dated page governsPrinciple is sound, but neither page publishes a revision date for the specification in question, so the rule cannot be applied from the evidence the site has.
  • CShow both and state no ruleCurrent state. Maximally cautious, and the least useful to a reader about to spend money.

Who should the site say wrote it, given that a pipeline writes and a human gates?

DS-10Authorship and trustAWAITING_DECISIONBlocked by: DQ-08

Publish a desk-level provenance statement and an author page that says exactly what the pipeline does, what the gates reject, what a human decides, and what the desk has never attempted (no hands-on testing, no ratings). Attribute the work to the desk, not to an invented person, and never to a stock photograph of a writer.

Why: Google's guidance asks who created the content and how, warns that fabricated creator profiles are deception, and recommends explaining automation (S56, S57). The honest version is also the more interesting read, which is the best answer to the 'AI slop' complaint.

  • ADesk-level provenance page, no invented person recommendedTruthful and cheap, and satisfies the 'Who/How/Why' questions. Some readers weight a named human more heavily than a desk, so it may convert slightly worse than a real byline.
  • BName a real accountable person (the owner)Strongest trust signal if the owner is willing, and it puts a real reputation behind every page. Creates a personal liability and maintenance burden the owner may not want.
  • CInvent an author persona with a generated photographNot recommended in any form. Google names fabricated creator profiles, made-up names and AI-generated headshots as deception (S56).

How should the site measure clicks, conversions and what to write next, with no account and no analytics?

DS-11MeasurementAWAITING_DECISIONBlocked by: DQ-01, DQ-04

Ship a documented measurement layer that is policy-safe by construction: a static, cookie-free, first-party event schema for outbound-link clicks (no personal data, no cross-site identifier, no user-derived sub-tag), a Search Console verification file the owner activates, and a conversion path that reads Associates reporting once the account exists. Publish the schema and the decisions it will support; publish no traffic or earnings forecast.

Why: Every growth decision in this register is currently a judgement because nothing is measured (DG-11). A cookie-free click counter is the only measurement available before an account exists, and it is enough to answer 'which page sends people to which product'. S58 forbids presenting any expectation of income, so the layer records what happened and never projects what will.

  • ACookie-free first-party click events + Search Console + Associates reporting later recommendedMeasurable within days and privacy-preserving. GitHub Pages serves static files, so the event sink has to be an external endpoint the owner controls; that is the one real cost.
  • BThird-party analytics scriptFastest to set up and answers traffic questions well. Adds a third-party cookie problem, a consent question and a script this project cannot verify.
  • CWait for Associates reporting onlyNo extra work and no new technology. Gives nothing until an account exists, and gives no page-level behaviour even then.

How a suggestion becomes a change

A suggestion is a proposal, not an instruction. When you pick an option, the choice is recorded in docs/discovery-t40.json on that row's decision field, the row's status changes from AWAITING_DECISION, and only then is the work built — under the same gates as everything else on this site: every row sourced or explicitly blank, no price, disclosure on every page, links resolving.

Two things are intentionally not on this board because they are not choices: the required disclosure sentence and the rel="sponsored" qualification on affiliate links. Those are conditions of participating, and DS-05 and DS-09 propose only to lock them in place.
