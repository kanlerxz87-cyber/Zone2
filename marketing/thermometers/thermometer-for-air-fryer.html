Air fryers · informational search

Do you need a meat thermometer for an air fryer?

USDA's answer is yes, and it is not hedged: whatever the appliance, check the inside with a food thermometer. Here is what its air-fryer page actually says, the numbers to look for, and which of the two thermometers on this site suits a basket.

Answers: do you need a meat thermometer for an air fryer · read on 2026-11-19 · every line sourced

What USDA says about air fryers, specifically

USDA's air-fryer page says foods generally need 5 to 25 minutes at 350 to 400 °F, and then, without qualification: always check that food has reached a safe minimum internal temperature with a food thermometer.T5 · www.fsis.usda.govUSDA FSIS

It gives two reasons a timer is not enough. Overfilling the basket can cause uneven cooking and undercooking. And each air fryer has different cooking times because of its size and power — but the safe internal temperature is always the same.T5 · www.fsis.usda.govUSDA FSIS

One instruction is a flat no: do not cook raw, stuffed, breaded chicken breast products in the air fryer; follow the manufacturer's cooking instructions instead.T5 · www.fsis.usda.govUSDA FSIS

The numbers to look for

Colour does not settle it. USDA's thermometer page says a hamburger cooked to 160 °F, as measured with a food thermometer, is safe regardless of colour — and that ground beef can brown on the outside before it reaches a safe temperature.T3 · www.fsis.usda.govUSDA FSIS

Which of our two thermometers suits a basket

The CHEF iQ Sense listing names the air fryer in its title among the cooking methods it is for, and rates its probe to 1000 °F.T2 · www.amazon.comAmazon listing

The ThermoMaven listing does not say its probe may stay in food while cooking, and USDA advises against leaving a thermometer in food unless the manufacturer's instructions say it is built for that. With the ThermoMaven, pull the basket and check at the end.T1 · www.amazon.comAmazon listingT3 · www.fsis.usda.govUSDA FSIS

Our take — an editorial judgement, not a claim the manufacturer or USDA makes

In a basket fryer, checking at the end costs you five seconds — the basket already slides out. A leave-in probe earns its keep on the long cooks in an oven-style machine, where you would rather not keep opening the door.
