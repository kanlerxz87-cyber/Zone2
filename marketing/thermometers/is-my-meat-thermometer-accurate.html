Calibration · informational search

Is your meat thermometer accurate? USDA's ice-water check

USDA says most food thermometers read within 2 to 4 °F — and that any reading is only right if the probe is in the right place. Checking the first part needs a glass, ice and thirty seconds. Here is how, and what the two thermometers on this site claim about themselves.

Answers: how to check if a meat thermometer is accurate · read on 2026-11-19 · every line sourced

The ice-water check, step by step

  • Fill a large glass with ice cubes.
  • Add drinking water and stir well.
  • Put at least 2 inches of the stem in, touching neither the sides nor the bottom of the glass.
  • Wait at least 30 seconds and read it without taking the stem out.
  • It should read 32 °F. If it does not, adjust it the way the manufacturer's instructions say.
T3 · www.fsis.usda.govUSDA FSIS

USDA labels the ice-water method best for most thermometers. In the same calibration section it adds that if a thermometer does not allow manual calibration, you may need to replace it.T3 · www.fsis.usda.govUSDA FSIS

Boiling water, and why altitude matters

The second method uses boiling water, at least 2 inches of stem, and at least 30 seconds: the reading should be 212 °F at sea level. USDA notes that distilled water is needed for true accuracy, and that at high altitude water boils lower — by at least 2 °F and perhaps by as much as 5 °F.T3 · www.fsis.usda.govUSDA FSIS

Our take — an editorial judgement, not a claim the manufacturer or USDA makes

Unless you know your local boiling point, the ice-water check is the one to trust. Ice water is 32 °F everywhere.
