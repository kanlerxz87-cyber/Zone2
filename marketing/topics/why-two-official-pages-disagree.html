24 presets or 25? A field guide to spec-sheet drift on appliance pages

The Emeril oven's Amazon title says 24 one-touch preset options. Its own product page says 25 cooking pre-sets in one tile and describes a 10-in-1 oven in the paragraph below. The model number on the store page ends in 02A; the one in the Amazon specification block ends in 02X. This is not a scandal - it is normal, it is everywhere, and once you can see it you cannot shop the same way again.

Why this exists: We found these three drifts by transcribing both pages row by row for our own table. This article is the method, shown on the machine where we found the most of it.

Three drifts on one product

Note the third row: it is not a conflict, it is a gap. Sources disagree and sources are silent, and those are different findings that most write-ups blur into one. Why it drifts

A manufacturer's product page is edited by the brand's own commerce team and revised whenever the template changes; an Amazon listing carries a title written for search and a specification block filled in by whoever supplied the data, sometimes years earlier. Neither is wrong on its own terms. Both are documents with a history, and a history is something a buyer can read.M3 · www.emerileveryday.commanufacturerM4 · www.amazon.comAmazon listing

  • Check the count against the feature list, not the headline: 25 pre-sets and a 10-in-1 oven are two different claims about two different things.
  • Check the model number against the box, not the tile. A suffix difference of one character is usually a variant or a revision, and only one of them will be printed on your machine.
  • Where a specification is stated by only one of the two sources, note which one said it. The wattage above comes from Amazon's block, not from the brand.
