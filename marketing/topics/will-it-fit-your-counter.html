Will it fit? The exact footprint of all three, in inches

Return reasons are usually not performance. They are the cupboard, the splash-back and the height of the cabinet above the counter. So before the capacity debate: here are the three sets of numbers that decide whether the machine is going to live on your counter or in a car park queue at the returns desk.

Why this exists: Every figure below is transcribed from the product page, in both units where the page gives both, with the weight that has to be lifted onto a shelf. The blanks are labelled, and one of them is the number shoppers ask for most.

Dimensions and weight, as published

The three questions the pages do not answer

  • How much clearance the manufacturer wants around the vents - not stated on any of the three product pages.
  • Whether the door swing needs front clearance on the 26 Qt oven - the page describes dual French doors opening for access, and states no swing radius.
  • How the published depth of 16.9 in relates to a real counter: the Emeril is the deepest of the three by 3.5 in against the 6 Qt model, and a standard counter is not generous with that difference.

Our take — an editorial judgement, not a claim the manufacturer makes

The 6 Qt is the one to put on a short shelf: 10.63 in tall, against 13.5 in on the 8 Qt and 14.8 in on the oven. Weight is nearly a wash - 13.3 lb against 12.8 lb, with the oven unpublished - so if the machine is going up and down between uses, the height number matters more than the scale.
