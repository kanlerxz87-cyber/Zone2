Using an air fryer safely

The Safe Minimum Internal Temperature Chart for Air Frying

One chart, every food, and the thermometer reading that actually decides when dinner is done.

Published 2026-11-15 · 6 min read · Topic: Using an air fryer safely

Short answer

Always use a food thermometer to check whether meat has reached a safe minimum internal temperature hot enough to kill harmful germs that cause food poisoning. Some meats also need rest time after cooking, because rest time allows the innermost parts and juices to become fully and safely cooked.

The instrument, not the recipe

The published instruction is short: always use a food thermometer to check whether meat has reached a safe minimum internal temperature that is hot enough to kill harmful germs that cause food poisoning.

That is why an air fryer recipe written by someone else is a starting point rather than a guarantee. The machine's size and power change how long the food takes; they do not change what safe looks like.

S42 · www.foodsafety.govUS government

Rest time is part of the instruction

Some meats need rest time after cooking. Rest time is important for certain meats because it allows the innermost parts and juices of the meats to become fully and safely cooked.

In the chart below, the rest time is printed with the temperature rather than in a footnote, because a 145 °F reading that is served immediately is not the same thing as a 145 °F reading that has rested three minutes.

S42 · www.foodsafety.govUS government

How the readings are grouped

Whole cuts of beef, pork, veal, goat and lamb take 145 °F with a three-minute rest. Ground meat and sausage take 160 °F. Poultry takes 165 °F, whether it is a whole bird, a breast, a thigh, ground poultry or stuffing.

Seafood is 145 °F, or the point where the flesh is no longer translucent and separates easily with a fork. Shellfish are done when the flesh is pearly or white and opaque, and bivalves open during cooking.

S42 · www.foodsafety.govUS government

Leftovers have their own number

Leftovers of any type are reheated to 165 °F (74 °C). This is a separate line in the chart because reheating is a distinct safety question, not a repeat of the original cooking.

Precooked ham that is being reheated is also 165 °F, with the note that cooked hams packaged in USDA-inspected plants should be reheated to 140 °F (60 °C).

Questions people actually ask

Do I really need a thermometer?
Yes. The published guidance is to always use a food thermometer to check whether meat has reached a safe minimum internal temperature that is hot enough to kill harmful germs that cause food poisoning.
S42 · www.foodsafety.govUS government
Does a rest time count towards cooking?
No — the rest is additional. Rest time matters for certain meats because it allows the innermost parts and juices to become fully and safely cooked, so it is printed with the temperature rather than left to the reader.
S42 · www.foodsafety.govUS government
What temperature do I reheat leftovers to?
165 °F (74 °C). Leftovers of any type carry that number in the chart.
S42 · www.foodsafety.govUS government
