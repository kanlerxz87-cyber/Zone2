Air-Frying Frozen Food: What to Follow, and What to Measure

The package says the time. The thermometer says when it is done. Those are two different jobs, and only one of them is a safety check.

Published 2026-11-15 · 5 min read · Topic: Cooking in an air fryer

Short answer

When available, follow the manufacturer's instructions on the food package, and always check that the food has reached a safe minimum internal temperature with a food thermometer. The package gives you the time; the thermometer gives you the answer.

The package instruction wins

The published guidance is unambiguous about priority: when available, consumers should follow manufacturer's instructions on food packages to ensure food is safely cooked.

Frozen food is the case where that matters most, because the package states a time for a specific appliance and a specific portion size. It is also the case where people most often ignore it, because the box says 12 minutes and the recipe says 15.

S41 · www.fsis.usda.govUS government

The window when the package is silent

Foods generally require 5 to 25 minutes of cooking at a temperature range of 350 degrees F to 400 degrees F. That is the fallback when the package says nothing.

Note what this is not: it is not a per-food recipe, and it is not a doneness test. It is the range within which air-fried food is cooked, and it is where you start.

S41 · www.fsis.usda.govUS government

The thermometer is the finish line

Always check that food has reached a safe minimum internal temperature with a food thermometer. For poultry that is 165 degrees F (74 °C); for ground meat, 160 °F (71 °C); for whole cuts of beef, pork, veal and lamb, 145 °F (63 °C) with a 3-minute rest time.

Frozen food is where this bites hardest, because the outside can be over-browned while the middle is still cold — a failure mode a timer cannot see.

S41 · www.fsis.usda.govUS government S42 · www.foodsafety.govUS government

The one exception, and it is not about frozen at all

For raw, stuffed, breaded chicken breast products, the published instruction is not to cook them in the air fryer, and to follow the manufacturer's cooking instructions instead.

This is not a frozen-food rule. It applies whether the product is frozen or fresh, and it is the single clearest prohibition in the whole published guidance.

S41 · www.fsis.usda.govUS government

Give the food room to move

Air fryers generally don't have a lot of room inside, and overcrowding can prevent enough air circulation to properly cook the food. When air frying a large amount, it may be best to do so in batches.

Frozen food benefits from this more than fresh food does, because a crowded basket traps steam and the food is already wetter going in.

S41 · www.fsis.usda.govUS government

If it is leftovers rather than frozen food

Reheating leftovers is a separate rule: leftovers of any type go to 165 °F (74 °C), and cooked food is refrigerated within 2 hours.

Air fryers can be used for baking, roasting and reheating food without the food becoming soggy.

Questions people actually ask

Do I follow the box or the recipe?
The box. When available, consumers should follow manufacturer's instructions on food packages to ensure food is safely cooked.

Do I need to defrost first?
The published guidance does not address defrosting, so this site does not answer it. It does publish that package instructions take precedence when they are available, and that doneness is confirmed with a food thermometer.

Can I air fry frozen stuffed chicken?
No. For raw, stuffed, breaded chicken breast products, the instruction is not to cook them in the air fryer — follow the manufacturer's cooking instructions instead.
