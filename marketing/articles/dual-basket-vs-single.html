Dual basket or single basket? The difference actually matters

Two independent baskets, one basket, or a 26-quart French-door cavity: three different answers to the same question, taken from the manufacturers' own product pages.

The short answer

A dual-basket air fryer cooks in two separate cavities at the same time; a single-basket model cooks one. That single structural difference decides which of the two suits a household that eats two different foods at once — and it is the clearest difference between the 6-quart and 8-quart Chefman models.

What “Sync Finish” does — and what it does not claim

The 6-quart model publishes a Sync Finish function: both baskets finish cooking at the same time. That is the manufacturer's claim, and it is the whole claim. It does not say the two foods cook identically, that they can run different programmes, or anything about taste, and this article does not extend it.

The practical consequence for a reader is narrow and useful: if the two foods genuinely need different times, a dual basket does not remove the need to check the earlier one. Sync Finish synchronises the finish; it does not synchronise the recipe.

Which one to buy, in one paragraph

If two people regularly want different food at the same time, the dual-basket model is the one whose published feature list was written for that situation. If you cook one thing at a time and want the larger single cavity, the 8-quart model publishes more usable capacity for the counter space it takes. If you are feeding several people at once and want more than one cooking mode, the 26-quart French-door model is the one whose published capacity and mode list are built for a different job entirely.
