Air fryer or toaster oven? Pick by the job, not the hype

The mode list and the temperature range decide it. Both are published, and on this product set they differ sharply between models.

The short answer

An air fryer is a fan and a heating element working against a small cavity; a toaster oven does the same job with shelves and a wider box. The one product here that is sold as both at once publishes its full mode list, and that list is the most honest way to choose.

How to choose in practice

Read the mode list as the answer to “what will I actually cook?” Four presets is a short list and an easy interface. Ten named modes is a longer list that covers baking, broiling, proofing and dehydrating as well as frying — the modes that make an appliance useful on a day when nobody wants fried food.

The temperature range is the second honest filter, and it is not the same on every model: one of these appliances publishes a lowest setting of 75 °F and a highest of 500 °F, another publishes 200–450 °F, and the third publishes 200–400 °F. If you need a low-and-slow setting, or a very hot one, the published range decides the shortlist before any feature list does.

One caution about this product set: the Amazon listing headline for the 8-quart model advertises a 450 °F Hi-Fry option, while the same listing’s bullet list and specification block, and the manufacturer’s own page, give a maximum of 400 °F.
