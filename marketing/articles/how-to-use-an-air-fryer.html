Cooking in an air fryer

How to Use an Air Fryer: Temperature, Time and Batch Cooking

The published numbers first, then the range each of these three machines actually reaches — and why the thermometer decides, not the timer.

Published 2026-11-15 · 7 min read · Topic: Cooking in an air fryer

Short answer

Foods generally require 5 to 25 minutes of cooking at 350–400 °F, the package instructions take precedence when they are available, and doneness is decided with a thermometer rather than a clock. Everything else is specific to the machine in front of you.

The starting window

Foods generally require 5 to 25 minutes of cooking at a temperature range of 350 degrees F to 400 degrees F. That is the published baseline for air-fried food, and it is the right place to start when you are converting a conventional oven recipe.

The guidance also says that when available, consumers should follow manufacturer's instructions on food packages to ensure food is safely cooked. The package wins; the window is the fallback.

Then use the range your machine actually reaches

The three models here reach different ranges, and each figure comes from that model's own page.

The TurboFry Touch 6 Qt states 200–450 °F / 95–235 °C with a Hi-Fry function at the top of that range. The TurboFry Touch 8 Qt states 200–400 °F / 95–205 °C. The French Door AirFryer 360 states 75–500 °F.

So a recipe that asks for 425 °F is inside the range of two of these machines and outside the third's stated range. That is worth knowing before you start, not after.

Batch cooking is a capacity decision

Air fryers generally don't have a lot of room inside, and overcrowding the appliance can prevent enough air circulation to properly cook the food. When air frying a large amount of food, it may be best to do so in batches rather than all at once.

Two baskets help here in a way that a larger single basket does not: the dual-basket model's two independent 3-qt baskets can hold two different foods at once, with a Sync Finish feature so both finish cooking at the same time.

Doneness is a temperature, not a duration

Each air fryer will have varying cooking times due to the size and power of the appliance, but the safe internal temperature is always the same. Use a food thermometer to make sure your meat and poultry is fully cooked.

This is the single habit that separates an air fryer that works from one that is occasionally disappointing. The timer is a suggestion about the recipe; the thermometer is a statement about the food.

Presets are a starting point too

The 8 Qt model carries four one-touch presets — fries, chicken, fish and meat — and the manufacturer's page describes precise temperatures from 200–400 °F catering to each dish's desired crunch level.

The 6 Qt dual-basket model takes a different route to the same problem: a Sync Finish function so both baskets finish cooking simultaneously.

And then what happens to the leftovers

Reheating is a separate safety question from cooking. Leftovers of any type are reheated to 165 °F (74 °C), and refrigerated within 2 hours of cooking.

Air fryers can be used for baking, roasting and reheating food without the food becoming soggy, which is a good reason to keep the leftover in the same appliance rather than reaching for the microwave.
