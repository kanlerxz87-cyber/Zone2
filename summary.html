Questions to answer before scaling

Each question is a gate, not a placeholder to guess around. The linked register includes what is known, what is missing, who can obtain it, and the next evidence to collect.

Open all questions →
  1. For each of the five submissions, what exact product was intended? Record the ASIN, official listing URL, date and confirmation source; keep the redirect observation in its separate field.
  2. Which Associates account and marketplace owns the supplied tag, and does one test link per confirmed product reach the intended destination and appear in the authorized report?
  3. Where will the implemented first-party collector run permanently? Review infrastructure logging, 90-day pruning/backups and privacy jurisdiction. The current five-event contract is docs/measurement-t40.json; no event count establishes a person or an eligible session.
  4. Who is the named editor, what publishing cadence is sustainable, and will visuals be original, properly licensed or permitted Program Content?
  5. A thermometer category is now published from the two observed thermometer destinations, because the brief asked for all five links to be promoted. Are those the intended products? In particular, should the ThermoMaven link point at the newer model its listing now advertises (F-44)?

Suggested order of work

Recommendations are review items, not approvals. Nothing is auto-applied from this list.

Review options and trade-offs →
  1. Resolve intent first. Confirm all five product mappings, including the two thermometers now published as their own category.
  2. Validate one tagged Special Link per confirmed product. Check destination and Associates reporting inside the enrolled account before scaling.
  3. Connect measurement honestly. Reconcile the event definitions, choose a first-party endpoint and retention policy, verify Search Console ownership, then report only data actually received.
  4. Publish a short editorial pilot. Use buyer questions such as basket-versus-oven trade-offs, capacity, cleanup/counter space and multi-function appliance checklists as hypotheses. Add an accountable byline and original/authorized visuals; do not call topics “trending” without evidence or imply hands-on use that did not occur.

Editorial guardrail. The FTC says an endorser cannot describe experience with a product they have not tried. A sourced specification comparison is not a hands-on test, and the copy should keep that distinction clear. FTC guidance.
