Why every number here links to its source

Zone2 field notes · 2026-11-15 · the methodology, in plain language
Affiliate disclosure: As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.

Most affiliate sites are written backwards: the headline is chosen first, the specs are arranged to support it, and the reader finds out later that the "sale price" was stale a week after publishing. This site was built backwards from that. The rule that produced every page you've read is simple: a value appears here only when an official source states it, and anything unconfirmed is left blank and published as a flag instead of being inferred.

What that meant in practice

The brief supplied three short links that look almost like Amazon ASINs — except each is nine characters, and an ASIN is ten. A lazier site would have quietly "fixed" the codes or guessed at the products. We did neither, and instead settled the question with controls: an unknown code on the same short-link service returns a 404 instead of a random product, which shows the supplied codes are honoured lookups; a valid ten-character ASIN fed to the same service also returns a 404, which shows the codes are not truncated ASINs; and the per-link tracking token changes between days even though the destination does not. The chain of evidence is on the resolution table, reproducible by hand.

The result is a site that can tell you a great deal about what the links resolve to — three air-fryer listings, verified against their manufacturers' own pages — and nothing at all about which products the brief intended, because no official source says so. That gap is published as flags F-01 and F-03 rather than papered over. It is the single most important fact about this site, and it is the reason the product mapping is the first question in the questions register.

The Amazon rules that shaped the build

Compliance here is not a footer paragraph; it deleted features. Four rules did most of the shaping, all read directly from Amazon's own documents (re-verified live on 2026-11-15):

  • The disclosure — the Operating Agreement §5 requires the sentence "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases." clearly and prominently on the site. It is at the foot of every page, and a CI gate fails the build if any page drops it (Operating Agreement).
  • Prices — price display is restricted to links Amazon serves and licensed API data. A price pasted into a static page goes stale the next discount cycle, so this site prints none. You'll never see a stale figure here, because you'll never see a figure (Program Policies).
  • No price tracking or alerting — §6(y) forbids it, so the "pricing analysis" module of this platform is scoped to Amazon's published commission rates, not per-product price watching.
  • Link transparency — §6(w) forbids shortening links in a way that hides the Amazon destination, and reviews may only be displayed via Amazon's sanctioned APIs. Hence: no star ratings on this site, and product links go straight to the canonical listing.

What happens next

Two decisions unlock everything else, and both are yours. Confirming the product mapping (which products did those three codes mean to identify?) turns on the blocked content queue. Supplying an Associates tag turns the tracking plan into a launch checklist — without it, the supplied links cannot attribute a single sale, which is the honest reason this site is pre-revenue. The suggestions board holds the options and trade-offs; nothing on it gets built until you pick.

Until then, the site keeps its one distinctive promise: you can check any of it. Every table row carries the page it was read from, the verification log records each check with its method and verdict, and the source register lists every official page this site stands on. Trust is cheaper when it comes with receipts.
