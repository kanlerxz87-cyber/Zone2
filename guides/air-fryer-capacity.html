A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Quarts, basket layout and usable measurements: a practical way to compare capacity without made-up serving formulas.

THE SHORT ANSWER

Record the stated capacity, how it is divided, and the usable measurements you can actually confirm. Leave serving estimates blank unless the exact model's source states them.

First, write down what the number describes.

The front of a product name is a starting point, not a complete comparison. Chefman's dual-window page lists 6 Qt / 5.67 L, the stainless-steel 8-quart model lists 8 Qt / 7.57 L, and Emeril lists 26 quarts / 24.6 liters. Keep the unit and the exact model next to the number.

Our checklist adds a second column: the layout. A split-basket total and an oven cavity should not quietly become interchangeable just because both are expressed in quarts. This is why our comparison describes the format beside the capacity rather than awarding the largest number a winner's badge.

Use your usual food as the question.

Choose a few things you already cook. What information would you need to decide whether they fit: the width of a tray, the length of a basket, or the space available when two foods are kept separate? Ask those concrete questions of the exact listing or manual.

We have not measured usable basket area, arranged food in these machines, or tested batch sizes. When those details are not confirmed in our source review, they stay unknown. A volume figure alone is not a reason for us to promise a particular number of portions.

Source context: Chefman · M1 ↗ Emeril Lagasse · M3 ↗ Chefman · M5 ↗

Check whether you want to divide the space.

The Amazon title for the observed dual-window Chefman listing describes two independent 3Qt baskets. The manufacturer describes Sync Finish so both baskets finish cooking at the same time. That combination may be relevant when separate dishes are the reason you are shopping.

It does not establish that your preferred large item will fit either basket. If you normally cook one item that needs uninterrupted room, request the internal measurements before deciding that the combined total answers your question.

Source context: Chefman · M1 ↗ Amazon.com · M2 ↗

Take this checklist to the listing.

Record the exact model number, stated capacity and unit, basket or cavity layout, published outside dimensions, and any confirmed usable internal dimensions. Add the source and the date beside each. If an internal measurement is missing, leave it blank and ask for it.

Then record your own constraint separately: the space you have and the food shape you want to accommodate. Do not label a model suitable until the measurements and model instructions support that decision. A shortlist can be useful without pretending to be a tested fit recommendation.
