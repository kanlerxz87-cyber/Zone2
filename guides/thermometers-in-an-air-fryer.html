Air fryer + probe · Meat thermometers

Can you use a meat thermometer in an air fryer?

Yes, but the kind matters. One of our air fryers halts cooking the moment you open the basket, which changes how you check.

Published 2026-11-19 · Temperatures from FoodSafety.gov and USDA FSIS · Product figures from the Amazon listings, read 2026-11-19

Short answer: yes, but the kind matters

Air-fryer food is small and cooks fast, which makes guessing easy and getting it wrong easy too. A thermometer settles it, and the two kinds work differently in a basket.

With an instant-read: open, check, close

Pull the basket, take a reading in the thickest piece, slide it back. On the 8-quart Chefman, the maker states that auto shut-off halts cooking when the timer runs out or the basket is opened (Chefman's product page), so the heat is not running while you check. Whether cooking resumes by itself when the basket goes back is not stated on the page we read, so look at the display before you walk away.

With a leave-in probe: check the fit first

The CHEF iQ Sense listing names air fryers among its uses and lists it as air-fryer safe (“Yes”), with 4.75 in probes (Amazon listing). Neither maker says whether a probe fits under a particular basket or rack. Before the first cook, check that the basket closes freely with the probe in place.

Air-fryer food is thin: go in from the side

For thin food such as a burger or a chicken breast, FSIS says to insert the thermometer from the side (USDA FSIS). A digital thermocouple needs only about a quarter of an inch of food to read (USDA FSIS), which is why instant-reads suit wings and thin fillets.

Which air fryer pairs with which probe?

Any of them works with an instant-read. For a whole chicken or a rotisserie roast in the 26-quart Emeril oven, a leave-in probe means you do not have to open the doors to check. See all three on the front page, or compare them on one table.
