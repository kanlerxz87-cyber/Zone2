Seven questions to ask before buying a multi-function kitchen appliance

Ten modes sounds like ten appliances. Before you clear the counter, here is how to tell which of them you will actually use, and which number on the box to trust.

Counter Notes · published 2026-11-19 · sources re-read 2026-11-19 · how we check

A multi-function appliance is a bet that one box will replace several. Sometimes it does. Sometimes it replaces one appliance well and the rest only on paper. These seven questions use the 26-quart Emeril Lagasse French-door oven as the worked example, because it is the multi-function machine on this site, and the 8-quart Chefman as the single-purpose contrast.

  1. Which modes will you use every week? The Emeril lists ten: air fry, bake, broil, defrost, dehydrate, grill, proof, reheat, roast and warm. Circle the ones you would actually use. If it is two, a simpler machine may serve you better.
  2. Where will it live? Its manufacturer page gives 16.9″ deep, 15.8″ wide and 14.8″ high. Measure the depth of your counter and the height under your wall cabinets.
  3. Does the door need room to swing? Dual French doors open outward, so the space in front of the machine matters as much as the footprint.
  4. Which count do you trust when the pages disagree? The Emeril's preset count and its accessory count differ between the manufacturer page and the Amazon title. When counts disagree, read the list, not the number on the front.
  5. Is your outlet up to it? 1700 W at 120 V on the official pages. Check that against the circuit you plan to use.
  6. What does the warranty cover? One year, per the manufacturer page.
  7. Would a single-purpose machine do the job? The 8-quart Chefman has four presets and one basket. It does less, and that is the point.
