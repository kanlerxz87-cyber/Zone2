Food safety · Meat thermometers

Do you need a meat thermometer for an air fryer? What the USDA chart says

An air fryer's dial sets the temperature of the air, not of the chicken. The federal chart is written in internal temperatures, which only a thermometer can read.

Counter Notes · published 2026-11-19 · sources re-read 2026-11-19 · how we check

The short answer

If you cook chicken, pork or anything ground in an air fryer: yes, or at least it is the only way to know. The USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service puts it plainly: “Cook all food to these minimum internal temperatures, as measured with a food thermometer, before removing food from the heat source.”

The dial is not the doneness

Every air fryer on this site publishes a temperature range, and every one of them is the temperature of the air the fan pushes around the basket. None of the three official pages claims to measure the food. That is the gap a thermometer fills.

Air temperature is what the appliance sets. Internal temperature is what the chart asks for.

The temperatures that matter

For whole cuts of beef, pork, veal and lamb, the chart adds a rest: at least three minutes before carving.

Which kind of thermometer suits an air fryer?

  • Instant-read: pull the basket, take a reading, slide it back. Nothing has to survive the cooking chamber. Compare the two types.
  • Leave-in wireless: the CHEF iQ description says “air-fryer and deep-fryer safe”. Whether a probe fits under the drawer of a particular basket model is not stated on any page we read, so measure your basket before you count on it.
