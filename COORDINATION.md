### T27 — follow-up #1 (post-merge live read): a third surface with the same count defect — **F-29**

After PR #51 merged and Pages deployed, this session read the **deployed** pages (not the working tree) and found
one more instance of the F-17 / F-26 defect class, in a surface neither gate had covered:

| Surface | Said (on the live site) | Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pages/flags.html` heading | **"28 open items"** | **25 open** (of 28) | `tools/build_site.py` headed the register with `len(rows)` — *every* flag — instead of counting open ones |
| `summary.html` KPI | "25 open" ✓ | 25 open | `tools/build_summary.py` counted `status == "open"` correctly |

Two pages of one site told the reader different numbers for the same register. Three flags (F-25, F-26, F-28)
are closed, so 25 was right and 28 was wrong. **Fixed** by making `tools/build_site.py` use the same
`status == "open"` test as `build_summary.py`; both surfaces now read **25 open of 29**.

Recorded as **F-29** and closed. Note the two gates then immediately caught the knock-on — adding F-29 made the
hand-written docs' "28 flags" stale — and both were corrected. That is the gates doing their job, not a failure.

**Lesson recorded for the next session** (three sessions have now hit variants of this): a drift gate proves the
*data* matches the ledger. It does not prove the *sentence* does. Reading the deployed page is the only check
that catches a false statement that was regenerated faithfully.
