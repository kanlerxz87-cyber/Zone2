Recommended path: small gates, then scale

Gate 1 · identity

Resolve the products

Confirm intended ASINs against account-side records. Keep observed link destinations explicitly separate from intended product identity.

Gate 2 · eligibility

Confirm the program setup

Choose marketplace, verify Associates status and tracking ID, then check current API eligibility and terms.

Gate 3 · evidence

Collect an auditable baseline

Capture sourced product facts, dated audience/topic research, and policy-compliant price/review data only if authorized.

Gate 4 · pilot

Publish a few useful pages

Use clear disclosures, cited trade-offs, an editorial review, and tagged links. Keep “trending” and “high-converting” out until measured.

Gate 5 · learn

Measure before optimizing

Compare dated traffic/click and Associates reports. Change one page element at a time and preserve the evidence for each choice.

Gate 6 · automate

Scale only repeatable work

Automate the checks that pass consistently; retain human approval for novel claims, identity conflicts, policy exceptions, and rollback.

The gates are project recommendations, not Amazon requirements. Amazon’s Agreement expressly disclaims a representation about expected traffic or commission income: Operating Agreement §4.

Suggested design prompt for the next site iteration

Build a calm, editorial-first affiliate research and buying-guide site for a clearly defined audience. Lead with the reader’s decision, not generic AI claims. Use concise writing, original reporting only when real notes exist, sourced comparison tables, clear trade-offs, “who this is for / who should skip it,” visible source dates, and an always-clear affiliate disclosure. Keep a decision dashboard for verified facts, missing evidence, and review flags. Do not invent reviews, prices, search volume, popularity, conversion rates, awards, firsthand testing, or product identity. Do not publish a product recommendation or affiliate link until its intended ASIN, current source data, and Associates tag are verified. Treat “trending” and “high-converting” as measured claims that need dated evidence. Respect Amazon’s restrictions on price tracking/alerts and review display. Make human approval visible for unresolved or policy-sensitive work; automate repeatable checks, not unsupported conclusions.
